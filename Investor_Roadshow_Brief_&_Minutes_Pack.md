## Section 1 — Five-slide institutional fixed-income briefing

### Slide 1 — Fiscal performance improves; ratings retain differentiated outlooks

**Executive headline:** The FY2025/26 primary surplus reached 4.9% of GDP, above the 4.0% budget, with a 5.0% target for FY2026/27.

**Key data table — percent of GDP**

| Measure | FY2024/25 actual | FY2025/26 budget | FY2025/26 outcome statement | FY2026/27 budget |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Primary surplus | 3.52% | 4.0% | **4.9%** | **5.0%** |
| Overall deficit, positive magnitude | 7.22% | 7.3% | **5.8%** | **4.9%** |

**Rating callouts — as reported by MoF before the cut-off**

| Agency | Rating | Outlook | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moody’s | **B3** | **Positive** | June 2026 bulletin p.3; reports upgrade from Caa1; exact action date not established |
| S&P | **B/B** | **Stable** | MoF statement, 10 October 2026 |
| Fitch | **B** | **Stable** | MoF statement, 8 October 2026 |

**Visual callout:** +0.9 percentage points of primary-surplus performance against the FY2025/26 budget. The FY2026/27 figure is a target.

**Speaker talking points**

- “The primary surplus strengthens the fiscal capacity to absorb debt-service costs. The 4.9% outcome and the 5.0% next-year budget are distinct observations.”
- “The FY2023/24 primary surplus of 6.14% included EGP 510bn of Ras El Hekma receipts. We do not use it as a recurring-revenue benchmark.”
- “Moody’s is reported as an upgrade; S&P and Fitch are reported as affirmations. A positive outlook does not guarantee a future upgrade.”
- “Exact FY2025/26 primary-surplus amounts remain unverified in this dataset. We do not derive a purported official amount from the rounded 4.9% ratio.”

**Sources:** S02 p.3; S04 Table 10; S13–S14; workbook Tab 5 E54:E78 and E115:E120. Agency information is MoF-reported, not a claim of direct agency confirmation.

### Slide 2 — Lower debt ratios and longer maturity anchor the medium-term plan

**Executive headline:** Budget-sector debt/GDP declined from a 95.7% historical peak to 81.95%; the MTDS aims for 71–73% by FY2028/29.

**Key data table — preserve historical and strategy vintages**

| Metric | Historical / strategy starting point | Latest outcome / intermediate projection | FY2028/29 target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total budget-sector debt/GDP | 95.7% in June 2023, historical series | 81.95% in June 2026, outcome | **71–73%**, 72.2% chart endpoint |
| Interest/GDP | 10.7% FY2024/25 chart; 11.0% competing text | 11.0% FY2025/26 strategy projection | **8.6%** |
| Total portfolio ATM | 3.5 years, strategy baseline | No verified June 2026 stock ATM in published statistical tables | **4.5–5.0 years** |
| Domestic share of debt | 74% strategy baseline | 78.77% calculated from June 2026 stocks | **80%** |

**Trajectory visual specification:** A debt/GDP line with separately styled historical observations at June 2023 95.7%, June 2024 89.4%, June 2025 82.5%, and June 2026 81.95%; dashed strategy projections at June 2027 78.1%, June 2028 75.2%, and June 2029 72.2%, with a 71–73% endpoint band. Label the series “Selected source vintages”; do not imply a uniformly restated series.

**Speaker talking points**

- “The ratio decline does not mean total nominal debt fell. Total budget-sector debt reached EGP 17,633.9bn in June 2026.”
- “June 2025 debt/GDP is 82.5% in the later comparison series, 83.8% in the MTDS baseline and 85.6% in the original bulletin. We preserve those vintages.”
- “The June 2026 outcome is 0.15 percentage points above the MTDS 81.8% projection, subject to vintage comparability. It is an ex-post benchmark comparison, not proof of causality from a strategy published later.”
- “At least 50% of divestment proceeds are intended for debt reduction; gross financing needs are targeted to fall by 9–11 percentage points of GDP from an approximately 40% starting level.”
- “The 1.1-year June 2026 new-issuance maturity measure is not the outstanding portfolio’s ATM.”

**Sources:** S01 pp.3–7, 10, 15–16, 27, 34–37; S02; S06; S09–S10. Workbook Tabs 1 and 3. No claim that all ratio vintages share an identical GDP denominator.

### Slide 3 — Separate the budget-sector stock from economy-wide external debt

**Executive headline:** MoF external budget-sector debt fell USD 2.43bn year on year; the USD 166.75bn CBE aggregate measures a wider perimeter.

**Key data table — June 2026**

| Measure | USD bn | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| CBE gross economy-wide external debt | **166.748** | Whole-economy aggregate |
| MoF external budget-sector debt | **76.148** | Budget-sector aggregate under MoF definitions |
| Difference | **90.600** | Arithmetic difference; detailed June 2026 allocation incomplete |
| MoF share of the CBE aggregate | **45.67%** | Calculated share, not an allocation of the remainder |

**Reduction callouts**

| June 2025 starting basis | Annual change to June 2026 | Status |
|---|---:|---|
| USD 78.579bn, located printed balance | **−USD 2.431bn / −3.09%** | Primary comparison |
| USD 78.618bn, narrative-implied balance | **−USD 2.470bn / −3.14%** | Conditional comparison; starting balance not independently located |

The printed-balance reduction exceeds the USD 1–2bn annual stock-reduction objective by **USD 0.431bn above its upper bound**.

**Speaker talking points**

- “The complete June 2025 arithmetic bridge is USD 3.415bn between the CBE government and MoF budget-sector aggregates, plus USD 79.236bn in central bank, banks and other sectors. These are June 2025 observations.”
- “The government-scope residual does not identify every entity or adjustment. We require compatible instrument, residence, valuation and revision information for a full statistical reconciliation.”
- “The MTDS domestic category includes EGP issuance and resident-held foreign-currency debt. A Eurobond is therefore not automatically part of the MoF external bond line solely because of its currency or international issuance.”
- “The Project 01 registry of USD 36.98447bn equivalent at 3 November 2026 exceeds the June non-resident bond line of USD 27.597bn by USD 9.38747bn. This is a cross-project diagnostic involving dates, instruments, FX and residency; it cannot all be assigned to resident holdings.”
- “USD stock reduction includes valuation and other stock-flow effects. It is not a verified cash net-repayment number.”

**Sources:** S02 physical p.14; S03 Table 46; S05; S06 p.11; S01 debt definitions. Workbook Tab 2 rows 17–34 and 47; F005–F010, F038–F039, F044. Project 01 registry amount is a carried input in this workbook, not a fresh review of Project 01.

### Slide 4 — Domestic refixing drives rate sensitivity; FX remains material

**Executive headline:** A high fixed-rate bond share limits immediate coupon repricing, while domestic rollover and foreign-currency translation remain significant exposures.

**Portfolio and financing controls**

| Measure | MTDS baseline / selected strategy | Denominator |
|---|---:|---|
| Fixed / floating external debt | **58.5% / 41.5%** | External portfolio |
| Fixed-rate commercial international bonds | **Approximately 93%** | Commercial bond subportfolio |
| Domestic debt refixing within one year | **74% text / 76% chart** | Domestic portfolio; unresolved source difference |
| International issuance limit | **Below 5%** | Gross issuance flow under Scenario 2 |

**Illustrative sensitivity callouts**

| Scenario | Result | Key assumption |
|---|---|---|
| +20% EGP-per-USD rate; no foreign-rate shock | External debt/GDP **20.88%**; annual external interest **EGP 269.53bn** | Constant nominal GDP; 6% baseline external rate |
| +20% FX and +200 bps foreign floating rates | Debt/GDP **21.05%**; external interest **EGP 306.82bn**, up **EGP 82.21bn** | 41.5% floating share; additional rate interest is debt-financed in the stock panel |
| +400 bps domestic refixing and +200 bps foreign floating rates | FY2028/29 interest/GDP **10.43%**, versus **8.60%** | Target debt composition; 74% domestic refixing |

**Speaker talking points**

- “The 58.5% and approximately 93% figures have different denominators. Neither removes FX risk, and fixed-rate obligations can reprice on refinancing.”
- “Every +200 bps of domestic refixing adds approximately 0.85 percentage points of GDP to interest, versus approximately 0.06 points for +100 bps on the external floating share. At equal +100 bps shocks, domestic sensitivity is about 7.13 times the foreign sensitivity in this construction.”
- “The combined debt result includes EGP 37.29bn of assumed borrowing to fund additional floating-rate interest. Without that assumption, the FX-only debt ratio remains 20.88%.”
- “These are model sensitivities, not official MTDS stress calibrations, forecasts or probabilistic cost-at-risk estimates. The rate mix is an MTDS baseline applied to a later debt stock; the model does not establish a fresh June 2026 mix.”
- “The controls support refinancing planning and transparent scenario discussion. They do not demonstrate that future issuance limits or maturity targets have already been met.”

**Sources:** S01 pp.18–21, 38, 40; workbook Tab 4 I9:I15, panels at rows 33–55. F027, F040. Simulated FY2028/29 composition: domestic debt/GDP 57.76%; external debt/GDP 14.44%, the latter derived conditionally.

**Visual callout:** Reserve stocks and annual flows are shown separately; they must not be added together as available cash for debt repayment.

**Speaker talking points**

- “The September NIR figure rounds to USD 57.35bn. The separately reported gross reserve figure is also USD 57.350bn after rounding, but the concepts remain different.”
- “The remittance series rose from USD 21.938bn in FY2023/24 to USD 36.460bn in FY2024/25, an increase of 66.2%. The later approximately USD 47.3bn is qualified by its partially accessible source.”
- “The adjusted FDI comparison does not remove all possible one-offs or establish a full cash/non-cash bridge. The unadjusted FY2023/24 headline is USD 46.065bn.”
- “A reported overall BOP surplus does not imply a current-account surplus: the same partially verified FY2025/26 announcement reports a current-account deficit of approximately USD 22.5bn.”
- “September 2026 import coverage is not established. June 2025 coverage of 6.4 months of merchandise imports must not be relabelled as current coverage.”

**Sources:** S03 Tables 12–13, 19–21, 30, 41; S01 p.32; S05; S15–S17. Workbook Tab 5 E81:E114; F045. Later values remain as-of-cut-off observations, not post-cut-off updates.
